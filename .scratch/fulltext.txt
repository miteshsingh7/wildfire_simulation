[0] Air-Traffic Conflict-Risk Advisory Agent (atc-conflict-advisory)

[1] Project Description:

[2] Air-Traffic Conflict-Risk Advisory Agent is an offline, advisory-only research prototype for investigating trajectory prediction, separation monitoring, and loss-of-separation (LoS) risk advisory algorithms in high-density en-route airspace. This software is for research simulation only and is not for operational use — it must never be connected to any live air-traffic control (ATC) or operational flight-management system.

[3] The project pairs a vectorized geometric conflict-detection engine with two kinematic baselines and a residual LSTM trajectory predictor, benchmarked with scenario-level train/validation/test splits and 95% scenario-level bootstrap confidence intervals across three calibrated synthetic complexity tiers (easy, hard_v1, hard_large) and an empirical live ADS-B sample collected from the OpenSky Network. A ConflictAdvisoryAgent wraps the full pipeline — history extraction, trajectory prediction, pairwise closest-point-of-approach geometry, and conflict classification — into a single Python API, and the AeroMetrics Conflict Research Console, a FastAPI-backed single-page web application, exposes the same predictors and live-computed benchmark metrics for interactive scenario replay and diagnostic inspection.

[4] Repository: github.com/miteshsingh7/Air-traffic-agent

[5] Scenarios

[6] Scenario 1: Offline Trajectory Prediction and Conflict-Risk Screening: A researcher loads a high-density synthetic scenario from the hard_large benchmark into the ConflictAdvisoryAgent. The agent extracts 60-second history windows for every aircraft pair at 30-second prediction origins, runs the trained residual LSTM to forecast 300 seconds ahead, computes pairwise closest-point-of-approach geometry, and returns a ScenarioAdvisoryReport flagging which aircraft pairs breach the 5.0 NM / 1,000 ft separation minima, together with the earliest predicted lead time to loss of separation.

[7] Scenario 2: Empirical Validation Against Real ADS-B Surveillance: An evaluator wants to know whether a model trained purely on synthetic trajectories generalizes to genuine flight dynamics. The live collector polls the OpenSky Network’s public REST endpoint over the Central European en-route sector for 20 minutes, reassembling continuous real aircraft tracks, and a dedicated evaluation script scores both the kinematic baseline and the residual LSTM against this empirical sample side by side with their synthetic-benchmark numbers, surfacing exactly where the learned model’s error grows rather than shrinks.

[8] Scenario 3: Model Governance and Live-Metric Verification: An engineer auditing the deployed Research Console opens the Model Benchmarks view. Rather than trusting a cached headline number, every reported precision, recall, F1, false-alarm rate, and lead-time figure is computed live by the same evaluation engine used by the command-line benchmark, with zero hardcoded metric dictionaries — giving a governance-ready guarantee that the number on screen matches what a fresh command-line run would produce.

[9] Technical Architecture:

[11] Figure: ConflictAdvisoryAgent Residual-LSTM Prediction and Conflict-Classification Pipeline (from src/models/lstm_predictor.py, src/conflict/geometry.py, src/agent/advisor.py)

[12] The technical architecture consists of five interconnected subsystems: the synthetic scenario pipeline, the vectorized conflict-geometry engine, the two kinematic baselines, the residual LSTM predictor, and the ConflictAdvisoryAgent together with its AeroMetrics Research Console.

[13] Synthetic Scenario Pipeline: Generates calibrated multi-aircraft trajectories (easy / hard_v1 / hard_large tiers) with maneuver injection, near-miss encounters, and Gaussian observation noise applied to history only; ground-truth conflicts are computed geometrically and cross-checked with a dedicated label-audit script, and each frozen dataset is protected by a content-addressed SHA-256 hash.

[14] Kinematic Baselines: cv_3step (3-step backward finite difference) and cv_smoothed (full-history least-squares linear fit), the latter serving as the residual LSTM’s zero-initialization anchor.

[15] Residual LSTM Predictor: A 2-layer LSTM (hidden size 128) consuming 6-dimensional per-step features (relative position and step delta, both scale-normalized), with a zero-initialized MLP head so that at epoch 0 its output is numerically identical to cv_smoothed; trained with SmoothL1 loss on the normalized residual, AdamW, gradient clipping, and ReduceLROnPlateau.

[16] Conflict Geometry & Advisory Agent: Vectorized pairwise closest-point-of-approach computation (src/conflict/geometry.py, src/conflict/risk.py) feeding one shared compute_conflict_risk() formula, wrapped by ConflictAdvisoryAgent into a single advise_scenario() / advise_file() call that returns a frozen ScenarioAdvisoryReport dataclass.

[17] AeroMetrics Research Console: A FastAPI backend (src/ui/app.py, src/ui/service.py) computing every served metric live via the same evaluation engine as the CLI, rendering a five-module single-page frontend (Conflict Prediction, Resolution Engine, Trajectory Inspector, LoS Advisory Ledger, Model Benchmarks) with a persistent research-only disclaimer banner.

[27] Milestone 1: Synthetic Scenario Generation, Conflict Geometry and Dataset Integrity

[28] Milestone 1 builds the offline research foundation: a calibrated synthetic trajectory generator across three complexity tiers, a vectorized geometric engine for computing ground-truth loss-of-separation, and integrity tooling (label auditing and content-addressed hashing) so that every downstream benchmark number traces back to a frozen, verifiable dataset.

[29] Activity 1.1: Calibrated Multi-Tier Trajectory Generation

[30] Step 1: Configure Airspace and Separation Standards: configs/default.yaml and the TrajectoryConfig dataclass fix the research airspace to a 200 x 200 NM horizontal domain between 10,000 ft and 40,000 ft, a 5.0 NM lateral / 1,000 ft vertical separation minimum, a 5-second sampling interval, a 60-second (13-step) observation history, and a 300-second (60-step) look-ahead horizon.

[31] Step 2: Generate Three Calibrated Complexity Tiers: ScenarioGenerator (src/data/synthetic.py) produces easy (500 scenarios, seed 42, pure straight-line cruise, noise-free), hard_v1 (500 scenarios, seed 1042, ~50% maneuvering conflicts with standard-rate turns and climbs/descents, near-miss encounters, and Gaussian history-only observation noise — 150 confirmed conflict scenarios, 0 accidental conflicts), and hard_large (3,000 scenarios, seed 2042, identical dynamics under an independent seed, split 2,100 train / 450 val / 450 test — 900 confirmed conflict scenarios, 146 distinct conflict pairs in the test split).

[44] Milestone 2: Kinematic Baseline Predictors

[45] Before any learned model is trained, two purely kinematic predictors establish the baseline that the residual LSTM must beat — and, per its zero-initialization design, initially reproduces exactly.

[46] Activity 2.1: 3-Step Finite-Difference Baseline (cv_3step)

[47] Step 1: Estimate Velocity from the Last 3 History Steps: ConstantVelocityPredictor estimates velocity as v = (pos[-1] - pos[-3]) / (2·dt) and linearly extrapolates forward. Being sensitive to observation noise on only two samples, it forms the "naive" reference the smoothed baseline and LSTM are compared against.

[50] Activity 2.2: Full-History Least-Squares Baseline (cv_smoothed)

[51] Step 1: Fit a Straight Line Across All 13 History Steps: SmoothedConstantVelocityPredictor fits pos_fit(k) = a + b·k by ordinary least squares over the complete 60-second observation window, filtering high-frequency observation noise while remaining exact on genuinely straight tracks.

[55] Milestone 3: Residual LSTM Trajectory Model

[56] Milestone 3 replaces the fixed kinematic extrapolation with a learned residual correction, designed so that an untrained model behaves identically to the kinematic baseline and only diverges from it as training teaches it genuine nonlinear maneuver patterns.

[57] Activity 3.1: Feature Extraction and Zero-Initialized Residual Head

[58] Step 1: Extract Scale-Normalized Features: extract_features converts each 13-step history into 6 channels per step — relative position (p_t - p_-1)/scale and step delta (p_t - p_t-1)/scale — using a 10 NM lateral / 1,000 ft vertical scale buffer, feeding a 2-layer LSTM (hidden size 128).

[59] Step 2: Zero-Initialize the Output Head: the final Linear(128, 180) layer’s weights and bias are explicitly zero-initialized, so that at epoch 0 the model’s residual output is exactly zero and its prediction exactly equals compute_baseline_cv — a verifiable, testable contract rather than an approximate one.

[62] Activity 3.2: Training Loop with Gradient Clipping and Early Stopping

[63] Step 1: Enforce Strict Split Isolation: train() asserts that the train, validation, and test scenario-ID sets are pairwise disjoint before a single batch is loaded, printing "CRITICAL" and halting if a scenario ever leaks across splits.

[64] Step 2: Train on the Normalized Residual: each step computes the target residual as (future_true - baseline_cv)/scale and trains with SmoothL1Loss, AdamW (lr=1e-3, weight_decay=1e-4), gradient-norm clipping at 5.0, and ReduceLROnPlateau, checkpointing best.pt on the lowest validation loss with early stopping (patience=6) over a 40-epoch budget.

[67] Milestone 4: Evaluation, Benchmarking and Empirical OpenSky Validation

[68] Milestone 4 evaluates every predictor on two fronts — trajectory position error and pairwise conflict-classification performance — first on the frozen synthetic test split with bootstrap confidence intervals, then against a genuinely live-collected empirical ADS-B sample, and reports the resulting generalization gap honestly rather than only the numbers that flatter the model.

[76] Activity 4.3: Live OpenSky ADS-B Polling and Track Reassembly

[77] Step 1: Poll the Public Live Endpoint: live_collector.py polls https://opensky-network.org/api/states/all over the Central European en-route sector (49.0°–52.0° N, 7.0°–11.0° E, > FL100) every 12 seconds for 20 minutes, collecting 5,359 state-vector observations across 118 aircraft.

[78] Step 2: Reassemble Continuous Tracks: 84 flights maintained ≥ 6 minutes of continuous surveillance, yielding 985 valid 60 s-history / 300 s-future prediction windows projected onto a local Cartesian plane — genuinely observed flight dynamics, not synthesized.

[79] Note (governance): an unauthenticated OpenSky S3 historical sample archive was initially retrieved but discarded, since it was not accessed through OpenSky’s primary authenticated interface; the historical REST API itself returned HTTP 403 (academic-account only), so only the public live-polling endpoint is used.

[80] Activity 4.4: Empirical Generalization Evaluation and Honest Failure Reporting

[81] Step 1: Evaluate Both Predictors on the Live Sample: run_opensky_eval.py scores cv_smoothed and the trained LSTM checkpoint against the 985 live OpenSky windows using the identical position-error and conflict metrics as the synthetic benchmark.

[82] Step 2: Report the Vertical Generalization Failure: the LSTM’s vertical RMSE was worse than cv_smoothed at every single reported horizon on real flights — 867.98 ft vs. 396.31 ft at 60 s, 4,334.14 ft vs. 2,778.97 ft at 300 s — while its lateral improvement shrank from +28.7% in simulation to +5.3% in the empirical sample. This is attributed to training on idealized piecewise-linear synthetic altitude profiles, which do not represent real barometric drift and operational flight-level changes, and is reported in REPORT.md as an explicit, unresolved limitation rather than smoothed over.

  [107.r0] def compute_conflict_risk( /     cpa_lateral_nm: float, /     cpa_vertical_ft: float, /     lateral_min_nm: float = 5.0, /     vertical_min_ft: float = 1000.0, / ) -> float: /     """Compute continuous conflict risk score P(risk) in [0, 1]. /   /     Defined as min(lat_risk, vert_risk), where each axis risk decays /     linearly from 1.0 at zero separation to 0.0 at the separation minimum. /     """ /     if lateral_min_nm <= 0: /         raise ValueError(f"lateral_min_nm must be positive, got {lateral_min_nm}") /     if vertical_min_ft <= 0: /         raise ValueError(f"vertical_min_ft must be positive, got {vertical_min_ft}") /   /     lat_risk = max(0.0, 1.0 - cpa_lateral_nm / lateral_min_nm) /     vert_risk = max(0.0, 1.0 - cpa_vertical_ft / vertical_min_ft) /     return float(min(lat_risk, vert_risk))

[114] Important Notes:

[115] Non-Operational Research Prototype: this software and its trained models must never be connected to any live air-traffic control, flight-management, or operational surveillance system; every view of the Research Console carries a persistent advisory-only disclaimer.

[116] Zero-Hardcoded-Metric Guarantee: every number the Research Console displays is computed live by the same evaluation engine used by the command-line benchmarks; an earlier static-shortcut implementation was found and replaced (commit 3d36b80) specifically to close this gap.

[117] Vertical Generalization Failure, Reported Not Hidden: the residual LSTM’s learned vertical corrections do not generalize to real OpenSky flights — vertical RMSE is worse than the kinematic baseline at every empirical horizon — and this is documented as an open limitation in REPORT.md rather than omitted.

[118] OpenSky Historical Access Restriction: the OpenSky Network’s historical REST API requires an approved academic account and returned HTTP 403 to this project; an unauthenticated S3 sample archive was initially retrieved but discarded under the project’s own data-governance standard, and only the public live-polling endpoint is used for empirical validation.

[119] Strict Scenario-Level Split Isolation: training asserts, at runtime, that no scenario ID appears in more than one of the train/validation/test sets, halting immediately if isolation is ever violated.

[120] README / REPORT Metric Discrepancy (Unresolved): README.md’s summary bullet reports the LSTM’s hard_large TEST F1 as 0.9230, while REPORT.md’s Table 2 reports 0.9425 for the same evaluation; both figures were found verbatim in the current repository and the discrepancy has not been reconciled as of this documentation pass.

[143] Conclusion

[144] This project investigates offline trajectory prediction and loss-of-separation advisory algorithms for high-density en-route airspace, evaluated strictly through geometric ground truth rather than any single fixed benchmark table. A vectorized conflict-geometry engine and content-addressed dataset hashing anchor three calibrated synthetic complexity tiers; a residual LSTM, zero-initialized to exactly match a full-history least-squares kinematic baseline, is trained to only improve where it earns its keep, and on the frozen hard_large test split it cut false positives from 56 to 6 and 300 s position RMSE from 2.9138 NM / 421.58 ft to 2.6798 NM / 148.90 ft. Rather than stopping at the synthetic result, the same model was scored against a genuinely live-collected OpenSky ADS-B sample, and the resulting vertical-generalization failure — worse than the kinematic baseline at every reported horizon — is reported in REPORT.md as an explicit, unresolved limitation. A ConflictAdvisoryAgent wraps the full pipeline behind one Python API call, backed by dedicated tests, and the AeroMetrics Conflict Research Console exposes the same predictors and live-computed benchmark metrics through a five-module browser interface with a persistent research-only safety disclaimer. A subsequent review pass unified a previously duplicated risk formula into one shared function, closed a PyTorch numerical warning, and grew the automated test suite to 95 functions across 11 files. Future work includes narrowing the vertical-generalization gap — likely by training on altitude profiles that better reflect real barometric drift rather than idealized piecewise-linear climbs and descents — pursuing authenticated academic access to OpenSky’s historical archive for a larger empirical validation sample, and expanding the Resolution Engine’s candidate advisories beyond the current fixed set.
